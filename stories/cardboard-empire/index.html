Questions

How much of Hasbro's profit comes from Magic: The Gathering?

In 2025 the division that makes Magic, Wizards of the Coast and Digital Gaming, earned $1,007 million of Hasbro's $1,140 million of adjusted operating profit, or about 88%.

Why are trading cards more profitable than toys?

A card is paper and ink, so it is cheap to make and ship. Players buy again with every new set, and because cards can be traded and resold, a sealed pack feels like a chance to win something. Toys need molds, factories and shelf space, and are exposed to tariffs.

What is the risk for Hasbro?

Scarcity. A collectible is only valuable while it feels rare. In November 2022 a Bank of America analyst argued that Hasbro was printing too many cards too often. Hasbro said it saw no evidence of overprinting.

The whole story

The narration of the film, word for word. Numbers link to their sources.

Cold open

Watch this pack of cards.

Inside, there is nothing but printed cardboard.

But on one day in June 2025, packs like this one brought in two hundred million dollars. 1

The company behind them is not a card company.

It is one of the most famous toy makers on Earth.

Monopoly.

Transformers.

Nerf.

Play-Doh.

But in 2025, toys earned barely a tenth of its profit. 2

Almost nine dollars in every ten came from cardboard. 2

From a card game that most adults have never played.

So how did a game, printed on paper, swallow a toy giant?

This is Hasbro.

And this is the cardboard empire.

The Numbers

Start with Hasbro's own numbers, for 2025.

That year, its sales grew fourteen percent, to four point seven billion dollars. 3

Now picture its operating profit as one hundred coins.

Eighty-eight of them came from one division. Wizards of the Coast, the home of Magic: The Gathering. 4

The entire toy business, every action figure, every board game, every tub of Play-Doh, earned about ten. 5

Now look at what each side keeps.

The card division kept forty-six cents of every dollar it sold. 6

Toys kept less than five. 7

One caveat. These are adjusted figures.

On paper, the toy division lost money in 2025, after Hasbro wrote down a billion dollars of its value. 8

And to be fair, toys are not dying. Toy sales fell four percent that year, not forty. 9

The problem is not that toys are small. It is that they barely make money.

The Game

So what is Magic?

It is a trading card game. Two players each build a deck, then battle with creatures and spells, until one of them runs out of life. 10

But the real product is not the game. It is the pack.

Cards are sold in sealed packs, and you never know what is inside.

Most cards are common.

A few are rare, powerful, and worth real money to collectors.

So players keep opening packs.

And every few months, a new set arrives, with hundreds of new cards to chase.

In 2025, Magic alone brought in about one point seven billion dollars. 3

That is more than a third of everything Hasbro sold. 11

More than thirty years after it launched, it had the best year in its history. Its sales grew fifty-nine percent. 3

The Rejection

It all began with a rejection.

In 1991, a mathematician named Richard Garfield pitched a board game to a tiny publisher, called Wizards of the Coast. 12

The owner, Peter Adkison, said no. It would cost too much to make.

He asked for something else. Something cheap, portable, and quick to play.

Garfield came back with Magic.

It went on sale in the summer of 1993.

The company had printed roughly two and a half million cards. Enough, it thought, to last the year. 13

They sold out within weeks.

Players did not just play it. They traded it, collected it, and argued about it.

A new kind of product had been born. A game you could never quite finish buying.

The Side Bet

Six years later, Hasbro came calling.

In 1999, it bought Wizards of the Coast for about three hundred and twenty-five million dollars. 14

Remember that number.

The deal came with two bonuses. Wizards also owned Dungeons & Dragons.

And at the time, it was the company printing Pokémon cards in English. 12

For a toy giant, it still looked like a side bet.

In 2025, Magic earned that purchase price back, in revenue, about every ten weeks. 15

And in 2022, Hasbro gave its top job to Chris Cocks. The man who had been running the card business. 16

Paper Beats Plastic

Why is cardboard such a better business than plastic? Three reasons.

One. It is cheap to make. A card is paper and ink.

A toy needs molds, factories, shipping containers, and shelf space.

Toys are also exposed to tariffs. Hasbro tied its billion dollar write-down to exactly that. 17

Two. Players come back. A toy is bought once.

A card game is bought again, with every new set.

Three. The cards hold value.

Because players can trade and resell them, a pack feels less like a purchase, and more like a lottery ticket you can play with.

The Crossovers

Then Hasbro poured fuel on the fire. Crossovers.

It began printing other people's worlds onto Magic cards.

The Lord of the Rings.

Marvel.

Final Fantasy.

Fans who had never touched Magic suddenly had a reason to buy a pack.

The Lord of the Rings set took six months to reach two hundred million dollars in sales. 18

In June 2025, the Final Fantasy set did it in a single day. 19

Six months. Against one day.

Hasbro's chief executive told investors the company still could not print enough. 20

In the last three months of 2025, Magic sales more than doubled. 21

Crossovers are only one of three engines.

The second is the back catalog. Older sets keep selling long after release, and Hasbro says those sales hit a record in 2025. 3

The third is a direct line to fans, called Secret Lair. Small, limited batches of special cards, sold online, for a short time. 3

Limited supply. A deadline. And no store in the middle.

And here is the surprise inside the surprise. This is not a video game story.

Of the two point two billion dollars the card division made in 2025, about one point seven billion came from physical, tabletop products. 22

In an age of screens, the growth came from paper.

The Weak Point

But a machine like this has a weak point.

A collectible is only valuable while it feels scarce.

In November 2022, a Bank of America analyst downgraded Hasbro, and accused it of killing its golden goose. 23

The argument was simple. Print too many cards, too often, and they stop feeling rare.

Card prices fall. Collectors sell. Game stores lose money.

That same season, Hasbro sold a thirtieth anniversary set. Four packs, for nine hundred and ninety-nine dollars. 24

Hasbro replied that it saw no evidence of overprinting. 25

The critics have not gone away. Many long time players say there are simply too many releases to keep up with.

But so far, the sales have backed Hasbro. Back then, the analyst put Magic at about fifteen percent of Hasbro's revenue. 26

In 2025, it was thirty-seven. 27

Right Now

And the story is still moving.

In the third quarter of 2026, Magic passed six hundred million dollars in a single quarter, for the first time. 28

That was thirty-four percent more than a year earlier. And older sets grew even faster than new ones. 29

The card division kept forty-nine cents of every dollar. 30

The newest crossover, based on The Hobbit, is already the fourth best selling Magic set ever. 31

Around the world, about thirteen thousand game stores now belong to its play network. That is up thirty-eight percent in a year. 32

And Magic now sits on the shelves of almost forty thousand big chain stores. 32

And the toys? They are recovering. That quarter, the toy division earned about a hundred and seventy-four million dollars, helped by tariff refunds. 33

So the gap has narrowed. But about two thirds of the profit still came from cards. 34

There is one last twist, and it involves Monopoly.

Even Hasbro's most famous board game now earns part of its keep without a board.

A mobile game called Monopoly Go, which Hasbro licenses to another company, brought it a hundred and sixty-eight million dollars in 2025. 3

No factory. No plastic. No shipping.

The Idea

So here is the lesson, and it goes well beyond toys.

Put a character on a plastic figure, and Hasbro keeps about five cents of each dollar.

Put a character on a card, and it keeps forty-six.

The idea did not change. The container did.

A game rejected for costing too much.

A side bet, bought for three hundred and twenty-five million dollars.

And a card set that made two hundred million in a day.

So next time you walk past the toy aisle, look for the little rack of card packs by the checkout.

That rack is the real business.
