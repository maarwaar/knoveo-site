The HiddenPetEmpire

Who owns the big pet food brands and vet clinics, and why vet bills keep rising.

The short answerFour companies known for candy, toothpaste, coffee and cereal are also among the biggest pet companies. Mars, the maker of Snickers and M&M's, owns Pedigree and Whiskas and runs about 3,000 veterinary clinics. Colgate-Palmolive gets 23% of its sales from Hill's pet food. Nestlé's Purina is about a fifth of the company. General Mills paid $8 billion for Blue Buffalo. Americans spent $158 billion on their pets in 2025.

Questions

Which companies own the big pet food brands?

Mars owns Pedigree, Whiskas and Sheba. Nestlé owns Purina. Colgate-Palmolive owns Hill's. General Mills owns Blue Buffalo.

Does a candy company really own vet clinics?

Yes. Mars bought the hospital chain VCA for $9.1 billion in 2017 and also owns Banfield. Fortune reports that it runs about 3,000 clinics worldwide.

What can pet owners do?

Find out whether your vet is independent or part of a chain, ask for prices before treatment, and check who owns the brand on the bag.

Cold open

This is Max. He is waiting for the vet.

Here she comes.

And she works for a candy company.

That is not a joke.

The company behind Snickers, and M&M's, runs about three thousand animal hospitals. 1

A toothpaste company makes a fortune from dog food.

So does a coffee company.

And a cereal company.

In 2025, Americans spent a hundred and fifty-eight billion dollars on their pets. 2

And the brands in your kitchen and bathroom want that money.

So why is everyone turning into a pet company?

This is your shopping basket.

And this is the hidden pet empire.

The Candy Vets

Start with Mars, the candy giant.

It has been in pet food since 1935, when it bought a British dog food maker. 3

Today it owns Pedigree, Whiskas, Sheba, and many more.

Then it went further than food.

In 2017, it paid nine point one billion dollars for a hospital chain called VCA. 4

Add Banfield, with more than a thousand hospitals, many of them inside PetSmart stores. 5

And in 2018, another two point four billion dollars, for a chain in Europe. 1

The total is now about three thousand clinics, around the world. 1

Fortune counts roughly thirty acquisitions since the 1990s. 1

Over those three decades, Mars grew from about thirteen billion dollars in sales, to about fifty billion. 6

According to figures cited by Fortune, corporations now own about one in five American vet clinics. 1

And Mars owns nearly half of those. 1

So the same company can sell the food in your dog's bowl, and employ the vet who examines him.

The Toothpaste

Now look at Colgate-Palmolive. The toothpaste company.

In 2025, it sold a record twenty point four billion dollars of goods. 7

It sells about four of every ten tubes of toothpaste on Earth. 8

But that is not where the growth is.

Picture every hundred dollars of its sales as one hundred coins.

Twenty-three of them are not toothpaste, soap, or detergent. They are pet food, sold under the name Hill's. 9

That is more than four and a half billion dollars a year. 10

And it is one of the most profitable parts. Hill's keeps more than twenty cents of every dollar it sells, as operating profit. 11

Its own annual report calls Hill's the number one brand recommended by vets in the United States. 7

The story starts in 1939, with a guide dog named Buddy. 12

Buddy had kidney disease. A veterinarian, Doctor Mark Morris, designed a special diet to keep her alive. 12

In 1948, he teamed up with a packing company in Topeka, Kansas, to make it at scale. 12

And in 1976, Colgate bought the business.

For a toothpaste maker, it looked like a strange side bet.

Today, Colgate's own executives call it the biggest contributor to the company's growth over its last five year plan. 13

Why Pet Food

So what makes pet food such a good business? Three things.

First, the vet. Hill's is sold through veterinarians and pet stores in more than eighty countries. 7

When a vet recommends a food, most owners listen.

Second, the calendar. A dog that eats twice a day for twelve years gets through almost nine thousand meals.

Usually of the same brand.

Third, pricing. In the middle of 2025, almost all of the growth at Hill's came from higher prices, not from selling more. 14

Owners are slow to trade down on a member of the family.

When money is tight, owners protect food and vet care, and cut the extras instead. 2

Coffee & Cereal

Nestlé tells the same story, on a bigger scale.

You know it for coffee and chocolate. But its Purina pet care business brought in more than eighteen billion Swiss francs in 2025. 15

That is about one fifth of the whole company, and its second largest category. 15

It is also more profitable. Pet care earned a margin of almost twenty-two percent, against sixteen percent for Nestlé overall. 16

There is a fourth name on this list. General Mills, the company behind Cheerios.

In 2018, it paid eight billion dollars for a pet food brand called Blue Buffalo. 17

It borrowed six billion dollars to do it. 18

Today, pet food brings in about two point six billion dollars a year. Roughly one seventh of everything General Mills sells. 19

That is four companies, from four different aisles, all arriving at the pet shelf.

The Money

Why all of them? Follow the money.

Their famous products grow slowly. People only brush their teeth so many times a day.

But spending on pets keeps finding new room.

In 2018, Americans spent about ninety billion dollars on their pets. 20

By 2025, a hundred and fifty-eight billion. Growth of roughly seventy-five percent in seven years. 20

The industry expects a hundred and sixty-five billion in 2026. 2

Food and treats take the biggest share. Sixty-eight billion dollars. 2

Veterinary care comes second, at forty-one billion. 2

Supplies, medicines and live animals add another thirty-four billion. 2

And services, like grooming, boarding and insurance, add fourteen billion more. 2

About half of the growth expected in 2026 is simply higher prices. 2

Food and vets. Exactly the two places where these companies planted their flags.

The Fridge

The newest battleground is the refrigerator.

Fresh, chilled pet food is one of the corners the big brands are now racing into.

General Mills says sales of its fresh dog food jumped eighty percent in a single quarter. 21

It now sits in about six thousand store fridges. 22

And Hill's, the Colgate brand, began rolling out its own fresh dog food in the United States in July 2026. 23

A product that needs a fridge costs more to make, more to ship, and sells for more.

Which is exactly the point.

The Vet Bill

The veterinary side is where the story gets uncomfortable.

In Britain, the competition regulator spent more than two years investigating vet services. 24

Pet owners there spend more than six billion pounds a year at the vet. 24

Its final report, in March 2026, found that average vet prices rose sixty-three percent in seven years. 25

That is about twice the rate of inflation. 24

It found that six large groups now own about sixty percent of British vet practices. 24

And that owners pay, on average, sixteen point six percent more at those groups than at an independent vet. 26

The regulator said some of the big groups were earning far more profit than a healthy market would allow. 24

Mars owns one of those six groups. 27

Fewer than half of the people using a chain even knew it was one. 24

The regulator did not break the groups up. Instead, it ordered every practice to publish its prices. 24

It capped the fee for a written prescription. It ordered a price comparison website. And it will make the big groups put their own name on the door. 24

And there is one more layer still forming. Insurance.

In the United States, more than ninety-five percent of dogs and cats still have no cover at all. 28

That gap is the next prize the industry is chasing.

The Catch

Two caveats.

First, this is not a guaranteed winner. A growing share of American pet owners say they are spending less. 2

And in 2025, Colgate's sales were held back by a drop in the pet food it makes for other brands. 29

Second, Mars is a private company. Its figures here come from reporting by Fortune, not from audited accounts. 1

For You

So what does this mean, if you own a pet? Three practical things.

One. Find out whether your vet is independent, or part of a chain.

Two. Ask for prices before treatment, not after.

Three. Look at who owns the brand on the bag.

A handful of companies now stand behind a great many labels.

So next time you pick up toothpaste, a chocolate bar, or a jar of coffee, remember what else that company sells.

The name on the label is not always the business.

Sometimes, the real customer has four legs.
